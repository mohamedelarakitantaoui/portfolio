<Chapter n="01" label="Problem" title="A risk score a doctor can't check is a score a doctor won't use.">

Rupture-risk models for intracranial aneurysms usually return one number. Three things go wrong with that:

- the score doesn't show **why** the aneurysm looks risky;
- a model trained at one hospital **may fail on another hospital's scanners**;
- a bare score is **hard for a doctor to verify** against what they see.

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="02" label="Constraints" title="One public dataset, one semester, and a doctor who has to check the answer.">

- **Training data:** IntrA, 2,012 3D samples from 103 patients, with its own meshing process.
- **External test:** the Lausanne TOF-MRA cohort, 50 subjects (25 with an aneurysm, 25 healthy), labelled with rough spheres, not exact outlines.
- **Trust:** a clinician must be able to check every prediction against evidence they can see.
- **Time:** one semester, working alone, supervised by Dr. Oualid Kafi.

</Chapter>

Each prediction comes back three ways: a **risk probability**, a **gradient-saliency heatmap** of the vessel regions driving it, and a **morphology report** of 8 measurements, from dome-to-neck ratio to aspect ratio. Every analysis ends in a PDF: the risk score first, then each measurement against its normal range, then a summary in plain words. One run takes 13.9 s on average: upload, segmentation and analysis.

<Chapter n="04" label="Key decisions and trade-offs" title="I chose checkable over impressive.">

- **Three explanations over one score.** I chose a score, a heatmap and a morphology report over a single probability, because a doctor can only audit a score against evidence. When the three disagree, that disagreement tells the clinician to look closer.
- **A patient-level split over a random split.** An earlier version split samples at random, so pieces of the same patient landed in both training and validation. I chose a 70/15/15 split by patient, which gives a lower but honest number.
- **One class correction over two.** A weighted sampler plus a weighted loss squeezed every prediction toward 0.5. I kept one correction, lowered the learning rate and waited longer before early stopping. That single retrain produced the 0.834.
- **A greyed-out card over a made-up value.** The size ratio needs the parent vessel, which the prototype doesn't segment yet. I chose to show that card greyed out with a note rather than display a number I couldn't compute.

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="06" label="Limitations and what I'd do next" title="It doesn't travel to another hospital yet.">

On another hospital's scans, run through my own pipeline, the ROC-AUC drops to **0.557**, barely above chance. The report traces two causes:

- **Different meshes.** IntrA ships cleaned meshes; mine come from my own segmentation, so every Lausanne subject is out of distribution.
- **A coordinate shortcut.** The pipeline skips the scan's full orientation data, so 21% of patients needed a fallback crop at the mesh centre instead of on the aneurysm.

Next steps:

- apply the scan's full coordinate transform so the crop lands on the aneurysm;
- harmonise the meshes, then train across several hospitals and scanners;
- add a doctor-facing “why is this risky?” assistant on top of the three explanations;
- release the code, the model and the validation protocol.

</Chapter>
