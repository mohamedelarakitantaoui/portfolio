<Chapter n="01" label="Problem" title="A risk score a doctor can't check is a score a doctor won't use.">

Rupture-risk models for intracranial aneurysms usually return one number. Three things go wrong with that:

- the score doesn't show **why** the aneurysm looks risky;
- a model trained at one hospital **may fail on another hospital's scanners**;
- a bare score is **hard for a doctor to verify** against what they see.

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="02" label="Approach" title="One prediction, three explanations.">

Each prediction is returned as:

1. **a risk probability** between 0 and 1;
2. **a heatmap** of the vessel regions driving the prediction;
3. **a morphology report** of 8 measurements: dome-to-neck ratio, neck size, dome size, tortuosity index, branching angle, sphericity index, irregularity index and aspect ratio.

The model was trained on **IntrA** (2,012 3D samples from 103 patients) and tested externally on **Lausanne TOF-MRA** (50 patients: 25 with an aneurysm, 25 healthy).

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="03" label="Architecture" title="From a DICOM scan to a plain-language report.">

The classifier is **PointNet**: 2,048 points sampled on the vessel surface → max pool → MLP → risk probability.
