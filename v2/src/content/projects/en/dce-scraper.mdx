<Chapter n="01" label="Problem" title="A chatbot needed tender documents. The portals hand them out one form at a time.">

Intelcia needed a dataset of public-procurement documents to train an AI chatbot. Those documents are the **DCE** (dossier de consultation des entreprises), the tender file attached to each consultation. They sit behind consultation pages and download forms on three sources: Morocco's public-procurement portal **marchespublics.gov.ma**, **marocao.com**, and Intelcia's internal purchasing portal.

The first version of the collector was one 3,643-line script. A run over 300 consultations took 2.5 hours, and when a download failed, nothing said so: the file was simply missing from the dataset.

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="04" label="Key decisions and trade-offs" title="I chose config and loud failures over clever code.">

- **Services over one script.** I split the monolith by job (find the URLs, fill the forms, download, file, retry), because in one 3,643-line script every step was tangled with every other.
- **YAML per portal over hard-coded selectors.** Each source is described in config, because portals change: a new portal or a changed selector is a config edit, not a code change.
- **A pool of up to 10 browsers over a sequential run.** One browser working through 300 consultations took 2.5 hours, so I ran them in parallel, with the pool size set in config.
- **Pattern-aware retries over blind retries.** The old failures were silent, so the retry service looks at how a download failed before trying again, and health checks flag anything that still fails.

</Chapter>
