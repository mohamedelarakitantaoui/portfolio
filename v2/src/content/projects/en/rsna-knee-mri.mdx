<Chapter n="01" label="Problem" title="58 expert labels for 4,407 studies: the labels came before the model.">

RSNA's 2026 Kaggle challenge asks for 12 probabilities per knee MRI study: ACL, MCL, medial and lateral meniscus, three osteoarthritis compartments (medial, lateral, patellofemoral), effusion, synovitis, Baker's cyst, contusion and fracture. The score is macro ROC-AUC.

Each study is a folder of raw DICOM files: up to six MRI series across three planes (sagittal, coronal, axial), with and without fat suppression, each 20 to 40 slices.

Radiology **reports** exist for every training study; expert **labels** exist for only 58. So the first problem wasn't the model: it was turning the other 4,349 free-text reports into training labels.

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="02" label="Constraints" title="Free GPUs, 58 labels, and data that can't leave Kaggle.">

- **Labels:** 58 expert-labelled studies; everything else is report text.
- **Compute:** free-tier Kaggle notebooks (2×T4 GPUs, with a weekly quota) and Google Colab.
- **Data:** the competition data stays inside the competition, so nothing on this page shows a scan.
- **An accident:** mid-competition, a laptop SSD failure wiped the LLM labels I had stored locally.

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="03" label="How it works" title="From raw DICOM to a submission file, on free compute.">

<Flow label="Knee MRI pipeline" steps={['raw DICOM', 'cache (150 mm crop, 288 px)', 'report labels', 'ResNet18 / EfficientNet-B0', 'offline inference', 'submission.csv']} />

**Report labels.** I built a labeller on the Gemini API: a radiology report goes in, a strict 12-field binary JSON comes out. I wrote three prompt versions and scored each against the 58 expert studies. Version 3 reached **82% macro balanced accuracy**, with 10 of 12 labels healthy and MCL sensitivity at 1.00. Turning off the model's default thinking tokens cut the API cost about 5×, and the runner resumes after a crash with per-row token accounting.

**The DICOM cache.** For each study the cache picks the series for 3 planes × 2 contrasts, orders slices by patient position, samples 16 slices, applies rescale slope and intercept, inverts MONOCHROME1 images and normalises by percentile. It ran over **4,407 studies in about 57 minutes** on CPU notebooks, so no GPU quota was spent: **21,137 series, 15.8 GiB**, saved as a private Kaggle dataset. Two studies failed, both truncated source files.

**Cache v2.** The first cache squashed whole frames to 224 px, so small structures like the menisci lost detail. Version 2 finds the knee by the centre of mass of its Otsu foreground, crops a fixed **150 × 150 mm** window using the DICOM pixel spacing, then resizes to 288 px: every knee at the same real-world scale.

**Training.** ResNet18 and EfficientNet-B0 multi-label models, trained on 3,479 studies and validated on 870, with the 58 expert studies held out as gold. The training labels come from a public LLM-label dataset built the same way as mine (chapter 04 says why). A small launcher runs one training process per GPU, so both models trained at once on Kaggle's 2×T4.

**Inference.** The offline scoring notebook copies the preprocessing byte for byte, and a parity check proves it: **99 of 99** series identical between the training cache and the test-time code.

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="04" label="Key decisions and trade-offs" title="A pass/fail line, written down before every experiment.">

I wrote the threshold first, then ran the experiment, then followed the threshold:

<Rows items={[
  ['01', 'Oct 8 · My model on cache v2', 'Needed gold ≥ 0.88 to keep investing; got 0.83. Switched strategy.'],
  ['02', 'Oct 10 · Blend my model into a 0.943 public ensemble', 'Needed ≥ 0.945; got 0.942. Dropped the blend.'],
  ['03', 'Oct 12 · Reproduce the versia-5 public ensemble', 'Needed to beat 0.943; got 0.954. Final pick.'],
]} />

- **Stop tuning the labeller at the data's ceiling.** The expert labels come from the images; my labeller reads the text. Effusion volume and synovial thickening show on MRI but rarely make it into a report. I tried a prompt fix for each, both made things worse, so I froze version 3 and wrote down why.
- **The 58 expert studies as a local proxy.** Their score tracked the public leaderboard within a few points, so I checked there before spending a submission.
- **Public labels after the SSD failure.** I switched training to a public LLM-label dataset built the same way ([stevenleehans/rsna-knee-llm-report-labels](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/stevenleehans/rsna-knee-llm-report-labels)) and used my labeller's gold results to know which labels to trust.
- **Speed alone couldn't win the Efficiency Track.** I derived the runtime-penalised score: about 0.01 AUC is worth about 12 minutes of runtime.

</Chapter>
